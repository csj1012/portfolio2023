#### Opinions are everywhere

Speaking of social media platforms...

For every ten people with Medium posts about the "so hot right now" framework-du-jour, there's ten more (okay, maybe two more) talking about how We Will Never Need Frameworks Again.

Who's right? 

Probably both people! 

Who should you listen to? Whoever you want, or whoever's saying what you need to hear at the moment. The thing I want to caution against is taking one person's word at gospel. You never know that person's background, something else, or biases. 

This goes double for people telling you about your own self or work... to evaluate that, advice for fellow non-confrontational people with a tendency to just believe whatever they're told, because others must know better:

1. Does this advice help me? Is it actionable? Is it even something I can control?
2. if it doesn't, is there still a takeaway the person didn't intend?
3. is this a person I respect enough to go to for advice? Are they doing the thing I want to be doing? Are they the type of person I want to be?
4. do I have a feeling they are wrong? Jot it down and come back to it later when you have a clearer head and have had time to think.
#### Best practices are vital, but you have to define "best"

What's good for a small portfolio site may not be good for a large enterprise institution's internal platform. This sounds like a no-brainer, but there are even people arguing about whether or not clean code is a good idea... 

Early on in my career, I realized that it was important to share a "definition of good" with the people I was working with directly. What constitutes "good" design? Is it better to have an easily readable codebase that new collaborators can understand at a glance but is slightly INSERT WORSE IN SOME WAY HERE? That all depends on your project, and getting clear in your definition of "good" is vital for success.
#### Identify when "good enough" is "good enough"

Yes, "done is better than perfect," and in 2013 I actually received the award for #1 World's Worst Perfectionist, which is a fact few people know about me.

But this concept, in all seriousness, is at the heart of the concept of iteration. What makes sense to iterate on now vs. later? Note: it is very important to communicate who will be doing the iteration later, lest people think you're implying _they_ should do it... (shoudl I even include this?)

Breaking things down into discrete steps and working on them is often the only way to approach large, complex problems.
