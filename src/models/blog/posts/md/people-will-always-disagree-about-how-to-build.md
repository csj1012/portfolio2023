#### Opinions are everywhere

For every ten people with Medium posts about the "so hot right now" framework-du-jour, there's ten more (okay, maybe two more) talking about how We Will Never Need Frameworks Again.

Who's right? 

My opinion is: probably both people, and also it doesn't matter! 

How freeing is that?

The thing I want to caution against is taking one person's word at gospel.

This goes double for people telling you about your own self or work... to evaluate that, advice for fellow non-confrontational people with a tendency to just believe whatever they're told, because others must know better:

1. Does this advice help me? Is it actionable? Is it even something I can control?
2. If the advice isn't relevant to me, is there perhaps still a useful takeaway the person didn't intend?
3. Is this a person I respect enough to go to for advice? Are they doing the thing I want to be doing? Are they the type of person I want to be?
4. Do I have a feeling they are wrong? Jot it down and come back to it later when I have a clearer head and have had time to think.

<p class="pull">It's vital to think critically about opinions you're going to take seriously.</p>

And on that note:

#### Best practices are vital, but you have to define "best"

What's good for a small portfolio site may not be good for a large enterprise institution's internal platform. This sounds like a no-brainer, but when online discourse exists to suggest that <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35105528">Clean Code, touted as fundamental reading, is maybe not always the best way to do things actually</a>, it can help to focus on your own needs.

Early on in my career, I realized that it was important to share a "definition of good" with the people I was working with directly. 

What constitutes "good" design? Is it better to have an easily readable codebase that new collaborators can understand at a glance, that is slightly less cleverly written? Is fast code the top priority? That all depends on your project, and getting clear in your definition of "good" is vital for success.

#### Identify when "good enough" is "good enough"

Yes, "done is better than perfect." And I should know. I'm a terrible perfectionist.

But this concept, in all seriousness, is at the heart of the concept of iteration. What makes sense to iterate on now vs. later?

Breaking things down into discrete steps and working on them is often the only way to approach large, complex problems.

Again if this sounds like common sense to you, you'd be surprised at how paralysis from perfectionism, or abstracting too soon, or making architecture decisions too early, can keep many people from starting their work at all.
